# PROJECT CONTEXT: DopplerNet Simulator Validation via VS13 Benchmark

## 1. PROJECT OVERVIEW & OBJECTIVE
The primary goal of this phase of the DopplerNet project is to validate the simulator's physical fidelity by benchmarking it against a recognized ground truth dataset, **VS13**. Since VS13 provides real-world acoustic recordings of vehicles at known speeds, it serves as the perfect "Ground Truth" for verifying the speed estimation capabilities of our physics-based simulation.

Specifically, we are validating the **Speed Estimation** accuracy. To do this, we obtained the "Base Audio" (stationary or source signatures) of 6 specific vehicles from the VS13 dataset and used our DopplerNet simulator's physics engine to generate synthetic passes that mimic the real-world recordings.

---

## 2. DATASETS DESCRIPTION
To perform a rigorous comparison, we have structured the data into three distinct categories:

### A. RealData (The Ground Truth)
- **Source**: 6 selected vehicles from the original VS13 dataset:
  1. Kia Sportage
  2. Nissan Qashqai
  3. Peugeot 3008
  4. Peugeot 307
  5. Renault Scenic
  6. VW Passat
- **Purpose**: Establishes the baseline performance of the pre-trained model on real-world recordings.
- **Samples**: 192 total samples across the 6 vehicles.

### B. ExtendedSimulatedData (Broad Range Validation)
- **Methodology**: Created using the base audio of the 6 vehicles mentioned above, processed through the DopplerNet simulator's physics.
- **Speed Range**: 30 km/h to 105 km/h (this range reflects the minimum and maximum speeds recorded in the VS13 dataset per vehicle).
- **Purpose**: To test the model's performance on a larger, synthetically generated dataset that covers the entire operational envelope of the real-world data.
- **Samples**: 462 samples (77 samples per vehicle).

### C. SimulatedData (Direct Comparison)
- **Methodology**: Simulated using the same 6 base clips, but the speeds are specifically set to **match the exact entries** found in the VS13 dataset.
- **Purpose**: Enables a one-to-one comparison between a real recording and a simulated recording of the same vehicle at the same speed. This is the ultimate test of the simulator's ability to produce "comparable" sounds.
- **Samples**: 192 samples (matching the RealData count).

---

## 3. PRE-TRAINED MODEL DETAILS
We utilize a state-of-the-art **SE-ResNet (Squeeze-and-Excitation Residual Network)** architecture specifically trained for acoustic vehicle speed estimation.

- **Architecture**: Deep CNN with SE blocks to emphasize informative spectral features.
- **Input**: Single-channel audio resampled to 22,050 Hz, converted to Mel Spectrograms.
- **Inference Mode**: 10-Fold Ensemble (The final prediction is the average of 10 independent models trained on different folds of the original VS13 training set).
- **Metric**: Root Mean Square Error (RMSE) in km/h.
- **Known Benchmark**: Achieving ~7.3 km/h RMSE on the full VS13 dataset.

## 6. RESEARCH APPROACH & METHODOLOGY
The core philosophy of this validation is to **"Isolate the Physics"**. 
1. We take the source audio (the sound of the engine itself) from the real VS13 dataset.
2. We simulate the **Trajectory** and **Doppler Shift** using the DopplerNet kinematics engine.
3. We pass both the Real and Simulated audio through the same pre-trained model.
4. The discrepancy in performance (e.g., 4.8 km/h RMSE on Real vs 26.8 km/h RMSE on Matched) indicates the **"Domain Gap"** between our simulator's current physics implementation and real-world acoustic propagation.

This allows us to identify exactly where the simulator needs refinement (e.g., air absorption models, ground reflection, or tire-road noise integration) to better match the features the SE-ResNet model is looking for.

## 7. FUTURE WORK & ALTERNATIVES: Retraining with Simulated Data
A key question for the next phase is whether we should retrain the pre-trained SE-ResNet model using the **ExtendedSimulatedData** we have generated.

### Would it be better to retrain?
**Yes**, but primarily as part of a **Hybrid Training Strategy**.

### Why Retrain with Simulated Data?
1. **Data Augmentation & Scaling**: While VS13 is a great benchmark, it is finite. The simulator allows us to generate thousands of variations (different speeds, distances, and environmental conditions) that are impossible to capture manually.
2. **Feature Alignment**: Currently, the model is "confused" by the simulated data because it was trained exclusively on real-world distributions. Retraining would allow the model to learn the specific acoustic signatures produced by our physics engine, bridging the **Domain Gap**.
3. **Pre-training & Fine-tuning**: A highly effective alternative would be to:
   - **Pre-train** the model on a massive dataset of simulated passes to learn the fundamental physics of Doppler shifts.
   - **Fine-tune** the model on the high-fidelity real-world VS13 data to ground its predictions in reality.

### The Alternative: Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs)
Instead of purely data-driven training, an alternative approach is to incorporate physics-informed loss functions. By forcing the model to respect the Doppler equations during training, we can reduce the dependency on perfectly matched simulated textures and focus on the underlying kinematics.

### The Risk
The primary risk of retraining solely on simulated data is **Simulation Overfitting**. If our simulator lacks certain real-world complexities (like multi-path reflections or varying road textures), the model may become "too good" at estimating speed from simulated audio while losing its ability to generalize to the messy reality of the VS13 dataset.
